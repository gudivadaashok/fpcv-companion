Boundary Detection, Interactively

Edge pixels are loose dots. To find a line, a circle or an object outline, you have to group them. The Hough transform lets every edge pixel vote for the shapes it could belong to. A snake instead lets a closed curve crawl toward the boundary.

02

Hough transform for lines

A line is \(x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta = \rho\). One edge pixel \((x, y)\) fits every line whose \((\rho, \theta)\) satisfies that equation, which is a sinusoid in \((\rho, \theta)\) space. Each edge pixel adds one vote along its sinusoid. Pixels on a common line have sinusoids that cross at one cell, so that cell collects many votes. Click an edge pixel to see its sinusoid. 03

Hough transform for circles

A circle with center \((a, b)\) and radius \(r\) satisfies \((x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 = r^2\). For a known radius, each edge pixel votes for every center that lies a distance r from it, which is a circle of its own. Votes pile up at the true center. With an unknown radius, repeat for many radii. Votes are scaled by the circumference, so a score of 1 means the whole circle is covered by edge pixels.

Selected circle

Simplification: every edge pixel votes in all directions, so a few false centers appear where circles and straight edges cross. Real implementations also use the gradient direction to vote for only two centers per pixel. Votes in a 3 × 3 neighborhood are pooled so small rounding errors do not split a peak. Circle coverage is counted exactly: edge pixels within 1 px of the circle, divided by \(2\pi r\).
04

Active contour (snake)

A snake is a closed curve of points. Each iteration moves every point to the best spot in its 5 × 5 neighborhood. The best spot minimizes \(E = \alpha E_{\text{cont}} + \beta E_{\text{curv}} - \gamma \|\nabla I\|\). \(E_{\text{cont}}\) keeps points evenly spaced, \(E_{\text{curv}}\) keeps the curve smooth, and the last term pulls points toward strong edges. Drag the dashed circle to move it, or drag its ring to resize it. Dragging restarts the snake.
