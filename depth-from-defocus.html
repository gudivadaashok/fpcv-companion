Depth from Defocus, Interactively

A lens focuses one distance sharply. Everything else blurs, and the blur grows with the distance from the focal plane. Measure the blur and you have measured depth.

The scene below is synthetic, so its true depth is known and both methods can be scored. Follows the Depth from Defocus lecture of First Principles of Computer Vision.

00

The thin lens and its blur circle

A point at distance z focuses at image distance v, but the sensor sits at vₛ, set for the focus distance. The cone of light cuts the sensor in a disc of diameter b. Change the lens and the object distance and watch b.

The equations

1/f = 1/z + 1/v vₛ = f·z_f / (z_f − f) b = A · |vₛ − v| / v A = f / N

Blur is zero at the focus distance and grows on both sides, faster on the near side. A larger aperture A makes depth of field shallower and the blur a stronger depth cue. Throughout the page, the blur is a Gaussian with σ = b / 2 pixels, a common stand-in for the true disc.

01

A scene with known depth

The texture is painted on every surface, and each pixel is blurred by the amount its true depth demands. This ignores the way blur spills across depth edges, so the real world is harder. Click any image on this page to follow one pixel.

02

Depth from focus: take a stack, find the sharpest

Take 15 photos while moving the focus from near to far. At each pixel, measure local sharpness as the sum of the modified Laplacian |2I − Ileft − Iright| + |2I − Iup − Idown| over a 7 × 7 window. The focus setting where it peaks gives the depth.

The x axis is the focus distance, spaced evenly in 1/z. With sparse texture, flat patches have almost no sharpness at any setting. Their curves are flat and noisy, and the argmax is a guess.

03

Depth from defocus: two photos are enough

Take just two photos at different focus settings. Image 2 is image 1 blurred by a relative amount: σr² = |σ₂² − σ₁²|. At each pixel, find the σr that best matches the two images in a 9 × 9 window, then invert the optics to get depth.

Errors skip a 6 pixel border. Two photos give a coarser but much cheaper estimate. Stack depth is limited by the focus spacing, and defocus depth by the blur matching and by where the two settings sit. Place the two focus settings on either side of the depth range for the best result. If the two are equal, the images are identical and the depth cannot be found.
