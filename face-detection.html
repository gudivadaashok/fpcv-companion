Face Detection, Interactively

A face is a pattern of light and dark patches. Slide a small window over the image, measure a few cheap rectangle contrasts, and let a classifier say face or not face.

Everything here is trained on small generated face drawings, so the detector finds the drawn faces in the samples. It will mostly miss photographs of real faces. Follows the Face Detection lecture in the Features chapter of First Principles of Computer Vision: Haar features, the integral image, nearest neighbor and SVM classifiers, and a sliding window.

01

Haar features

A Haar feature is the sum of the pixels under the white rectangles minus the sum under the black ones. Drag the rectangle over the 24×24 patch, or use the sliders. On the mean face, put a black band on the eyes and a white band on the cheeks and the value is large and positive.

Why these three types

A 2-rectangle feature measures an edge (eyes against cheeks). A 3-rectangle feature measures a bar (the bright nose bridge between two dark eyes). A 4-rectangle feature measures a diagonal pattern. Each is f = Σwhite I − Σblack I, with equal white and black areas so a flat patch gives 0.

The normalized value divides by s²σ, where s is the window side and σ its standard deviation. That cancels the window's brightness and contrast, so a feature means the same in a dim or a bright photo. Windows with σ < 2 are flat and report 0.

Integral image

The integral image S(x,y) holds the sum of all pixels above and to the left. After one pass over the image, any rectangle sum costs four lookups: A + D − B − C. Drag on the small image, or use the sliders.

Nearest neighbor

Standardize each feature, then find the k closest training windows of each class. With d+ the mean distance to the face neighbors and d− to the non-face neighbors, the score is (d− − d+)/(d− + d+). Positive means face. It needs all 400 training vectors at test time.

Linear SVM

Find the line w·x + b = 0 that separates the classes with the widest margin by minimizing λ/2 |w|² + mean max(0, 1 − y(w·x + b)). Only the support vectors, which sit on or inside the margin lines, matter. Testing is one dot product.

04

Sliding window

To find faces of unknown size and position, classify every window at every position and scale. The window grows by a constant factor, and the integral image means no image pyramid is needed: the same four lookups work at any size.

How many windows

With image width W, height H, window side s and stride t, the window can start at nx = ⌊(W − s)/t⌋ + 1 columns and ny = ⌊(H − s)/t⌋ + 1 rows. The sizes are si = round(base · stepi) while si ≤ min(W, H). The total is Σi nxny.

Detections

One face fires many overlapping windows at nearby positions and scales. Keep the windows that score above the threshold, then merge the overlapping ones into a single box.

Threshold and neighbors

A low threshold catches faces that fit the window poorly but admits false alarms. A high one is clean but can miss faces. A real face fires several windows (neighbors in position and scale) while a false alarm is usually isolated, so a cluster needs at least the minimum number of neighbors to survive.

Merging and cost

Hits whose boxes overlap with intersection over union ≥ 0.3 join the cluster of the best-scoring hit, and the output box is the score-weighted average. The integral image makes each window cost a few dozen reads regardless of size. As a one-line early rejection note: most windows can be dismissed after the first feature alone, and the chip below counts how many.
