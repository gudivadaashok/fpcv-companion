Image Formation, Interactively

A camera maps a 3D world onto a flat sensor. Open or shrink the pinhole, move the camera, slide the object, and watch light, blur and geometry change.

All scenes here are synthetic and drawn in your browser. Follows the Image Formation lectures: pinhole camera, perspective projection, and lenses.

The pinhole camera

Light from each scene point passes through one small hole and lands on the image plane. A big hole lets in more light but smears every point into a spot. A tiny hole gives a sharp ray, until diffraction (light bending around the edge) smears it again. Drag the diagram or the plot, or use the slider.

Perspective projection

A point \((X, Y, Z)\) in camera coordinates lands at \(x = fX/Z\), \(y = fY/Z\). Parallel lines in 3D that are not parallel to the image plane meet at a vanishing point. Click a cube corner to see its numbers plugged in. Click or drag in the top view to move the camera.

The thin lens

A lens gathers many rays from one object point and bends them to meet at one image point. Three rays are easy to trace: one parallel to the axis (it crosses the far focal point), one through the lens center (it goes straight), and one through the near focal point (it leaves parallel). Drag the arrow tip.

Thin lens equation

What to try

Put the object beyond \(2f\): the image is real, inverted and smaller. At \(o = 2f\) it has the same size. Between \(f\) and \(2f\) it is larger and farther away. At \(o = f\) the rays leave parallel and no image forms. Inside \(f\) the image is virtual (dashed), upright and larger, like a magnifying glass.

03

Aperture, blur circle and depth of field

The sensor sits where the focus distance is sharp. An object at another distance focuses in front of or behind the sensor, so each point becomes a blur circle of diameter \(c = D\,|i - i_{focus}| / i\), where \(D = f/N\) is the aperture diameter. A smaller aperture (larger f-number \(N\)) shrinks \(c\), so more of the scene looks sharp. Click a target or the plot to choose which distance the ray diagram shows.
