01

Sampling and aliasing

Keeping every k-th pixel is sampling at 1/k cycles per pixel. Patterns finer than half that rate (the Nyquist limit) cannot be represented, and they come back as false coarse patterns. Blurring first removes the fine detail, so nothing is left to alias. Click the left image to pick a row for the plot.

02

Quantization

Each pixel is stored with b bits, so only 2b brightness levels exist. Few levels cause false contours (bands) in smooth areas like the sky. Click the quantized image to see one pixel round to its nearest level.

03

Sensor noise

Light arrives as photons, so the electron count N varies by √N (shot noise). The readout circuit adds its own fixed noise σr. At low exposure read noise dominates. At high exposure shot noise dominates, and SNR grows only as √N. Click the noisy image to inspect a pixel.

04

Bayer mosaic and demosaicing

A sensor pixel measures one color only. The Bayer pattern (RGGB) puts a red, green or blue filter over each pixel, with twice as many green as red or blue. The camera then guesses the two missing colors from neighbors. Bilinear interpolation does this by averaging. Near sharp edges the guess is wrong, which makes color fringes.
