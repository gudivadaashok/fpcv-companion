Brightness constancy assumes a moving point keeps its brightness: I(x + u, y + v, t + 1) = I(x, y, t). The flicker view swaps between frames so you can see the motion.

02

Estimated flow

Lucas–Kanade assumes all pixels in a small window move together and solves for the single (u, v) that best fits them. A coarse-to-fine pyramid handles motions larger than a pixel or two. Click the arrows to look inside a window.

Color key: hue shows direction (red = right, toward yellow = up) and stronger color means faster motion. Gray areas barely move or are too flat to measure.

03

Inside one window

Each pixel gives one equation, Ixu + Iyv + It = 0, which is a line in the (u, v) plane. One line alone cannot pin down the motion. Lucas–Kanade finds the point closest to all lines in the window.

The least-squares solution

[ ΣIₓ² ΣIₓI_y ] [u] [ ΣIₓI_t ]

[ ΣIₓI_y ΣI_y² ] [v] = − [ ΣI_yI_t ]

The 2×2 matrix on the left describes the texture in the window. If both of its eigenvalues are large (a corner or texture), the lines cross at a clear point. If one is near zero (a straight edge), all lines are parallel and only motion across the edge can be measured. This is the aperture problem. If both are small (a flat region), nothing can be measured.
