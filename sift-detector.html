SIFT Detector, Interactively

SIFT finds blobs that look the same at any size and turns each into a 128-number fingerprint that survives rotation and scaling.

Pick a sample or use your own photo. Follows the SIFT Detector lecture in the Features chapter of First Principles of Computer Vision. This page implements the core of SIFT with a few simplifications, listed where they apply.

01

Gaussian scale space

Blurring with a Gaussian of width \(\sigma\) removes detail finer than \(\sigma\). Stacking many blur levels gives a scale space. Drag \(\sigma\), then click any image to read the numbers at one pixel.

The formulas

\[ \begin{aligned} G_\sigma(x,y) &= \frac{1}{2\pi\sigma^2}\exp\!\Big(-\frac{x^2+y^2}{2\sigma^2}\Big)\\ L(x,y,\sigma) &= G_\sigma(x,y) \ast I(x,y)\\ D(x,y,\sigma) &= L(x,y,k\sigma) - L(x,y,\sigma)\\ &\approx (k-1)\,\sigma^2\,\nabla^2 G \ast I \end{aligned} \]

The difference of two blurs approximates the Laplacian of Gaussian, scaled by \(\sigma^2\). A blob of radius \(r\) gives its strongest \(D\) when \(\sigma \approx r/\sqrt{2}\).

02

Difference-of-Gaussians stack

SIFT blurs in octaves. Each octave holds 6 blurred images with \(\sigma\) growing by \(2^{1/3}\) per step, so 5 DoG layers. The next octave starts from a half-size copy. Each row below is one octave, drawn at its true relative size.

Is this pixel an extremum?

A keypoint candidate is a DoG value that is larger (or smaller) than all 26 neighbors in a 3×3×3 cube across space and scale. Choose a layer of octave 0 and click a pixel. Dots mark candidates that pass the contrast threshold from the next section.

03

Keypoints

Each circle is one keypoint. Its radius is \(\sqrt{2}\,\sigma\), the blob size where \(D\) peaked. The line shows the dominant gradient direction. Raise the contrast threshold to drop weak blobs. Lower the edge ratio to drop points that sit on a line instead of a corner or blob. Click a circle to inspect it.

\[ \text{contrast: } |D| \ge \text{threshold},\qquad \text{edge test: } \frac{\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{H})^2}{\det(\mathbf{H})} < \frac{(r+1)^2}{r},\qquad \mathbf{H} = \begin{bmatrix} D_{xx} & D_{xy}\\ D_{xy} & D_{yy} \end{bmatrix} \]

On an edge, D curves strongly across the edge and barely along it, so the two curvatures differ a lot and \(\operatorname{tr}^2/\det\) is large. This page keeps the pixel grid location of each extremum. Real SIFT also refines the location to sub-pixel and sub-scale accuracy and starts with a 2× upsampled image, so it finds finer blobs.

04

Orientation and descriptor of one keypoint

Gradients around the keypoint vote into a 36-bin histogram weighted by magnitude and a Gaussian window. The peak becomes the keypoint's orientation. Any other peak above 80% of the maximum makes a second keypoint at the same place. Then the patch is rotated to that orientation and summarized as 4×4 cells of 8 direction bins each.

05

Repeatability: rotate or scale, detect again, match

The image is rotated and scaled about its center. SIFT runs again on the new image. Each descriptor is matched to its nearest neighbor in the other image, and kept only if it passes Lowe's ratio test: nearest distance < ratio × second nearest. A match is correct if the point lands where the known transform says it should. Only features visible in both views are compared.
